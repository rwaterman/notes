# Tasks vs Orchestration

The "will AI take your job?" debate usually conflates a **task** with a **job**. A job is not one task — it's the **orchestration** of many, and the switching between them is the hard, human part.

## The Argument

- AI is strong at discrete, well-scoped **tasks**.
- A **job** is a bundle of many such tasks plus the judgment to sequence, prioritize, and switch between them as the situation changes.
- Automating one task doesn't automate the job — it removes one input from a larger loop a human still runs.

## Example — The X-Ray Technician

Looks like a single task: *take X-rays*. Actually a stream of orchestrated tasks:

- Position the patient and the equipment for the specific study.
- Adjust exposure for the patient's body and the clinical question.
- Judge whether the image is diagnostically usable — reshoot if not.
- Handle anxious, immobile, or non-cooperative patients.
- Follow safety, dosage, and privacy protocols; flag anything abnormal.
- Coordinate with radiologists and the rest of the care team.

A human technician fluidly **switches** among all of these. AI can assist individual steps (optimize exposure, flag anomalies) but doesn't orchestrate the whole — so the job holds even as the tasks get automated.

## Takeaway

Automation reshapes jobs by absorbing tasks, not by replacing the orchestration. The durable skill is **orchestration** — sequencing, judgment, and adaptation across tasks — not any single task AI happens to do well today.
